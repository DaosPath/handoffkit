## Evidencia Markdown recuperada

### Sun y Abraham — arXiv:1804.05785

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05785

Título: *Estimating Dynamic Treatment Effects in Event Studies with Heterogeneous Treatment Effects*.

Autores: Liyang Sun y Sarah Abraham. Revisado el 22 de septiembre de 2020.

Extracto recuperado:

> To estimate the dynamic effects of an absorbing treatment, researchers often use two-way fixed effects regressions that include leads and lags of the treatment. We show that in settings with variation in treatment timing across units, the coefficient on a given lead or lag can be contaminated by effects from other periods, and apparent pretrends can arise solely from treatment effects heterogeneity. We propose an alternative estimator that is free of contamination, and illustrate the relative shortcomings of two-way fixed effects regressions with leads and lags through an empirical application.

### Borusyak, Jaravel y Spiess — arXiv:2108.12419

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12419

Título: *Revisiting Event Study Designs: Robust and Efficient Estimation*.

Autores: Kirill Borusyak, Xavier Jaravel y Jann Spiess. Revisado el 16 de enero de 2024.

Extracto recuperado:

> We develop a framework for difference-in-differences designs with staggered treatment adoption and heterogeneous causal effects. We show that conventional regression-based estimators fail to provide unbiased estimates of relevant estimands absent strong restrictions on treatment-effect homogeneity. We then derive the efficient estimator addressing this challenge, which takes an intuitive “imputation” form when treatment-effect heterogeneity is unrestricted. We characterize the asymptotic behavior of the estimator, propose tools for inference, and develop tests for identifying assumptions. Our method applies with time-varying controls, in triple-difference designs, and with certain non-binary treatments.

### Callaway y Tsyawo — arXiv:2308.02899

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02899

Título: *Treatment Effects in Staggered Adoption Designs with Non-Parallel Trends*.

Autores: Brantly Callaway y Emmanuel Selorm Tsyawo. Enviado el 5 de agosto de 2023.

Extracto recuperado:

> This paper considers identifying and estimating causal effect parameters in a staggered treatment adoption setting. We consider the case where untreated potential outcomes may follow non-parallel trends over time across groups. This implies that the identifying assumptions of leading approaches such as difference-in-differences do not hold. We mainly focus on the case where untreated potential outcomes are generated by an interactive fixed effects model and show that variation in treatment timing provides additional moment conditions that can be used to recover a large class of target causal effect parameters. Our approach exploits the variation in treatment timing without requiring either (i) a large number of time periods or (ii) any extra exclusion restrictions.
